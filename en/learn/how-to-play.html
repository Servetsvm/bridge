How to Play Bridge: Rules for Beginners

Learn to play bridge in ten minutes: partners, the deal, the auction, the contract, trumps and the play of the cards. Then play free against robots.

Four players, two partnerships

Bridge is played by four players in two partnerships: North–South against East–West. Partners sit opposite each other. A normal 52-card deck is dealt out, 13 cards to each player.

The auction

Starting with the dealer, the players bid in turn, clockwise. A bid names a number of tricks above six (from 1 to 7) and a trump suit or notrump: 1♣ promises 7 tricks with clubs as trumps, 4♠ promises 10 tricks with spades as trumps.

Every bid must be higher than the last one. At the same level the strains rank ♣ ♦ ♥ ♠ NT (notrump highest). Instead of bidding you may pass, double an opponent's bid or redouble their double. The auction ends after three passes in a row.

The contract, declarer and dummy

The last bid is the contract. Of the side that won the auction, the player who first named the contract's strain is the declarer; their partner becomes the dummy.

The play

The player on declarer's left makes the opening lead. Then dummy's cards are laid face up on the table and declarer plays them as well as their own.

  • Everyone must follow suit if they can.
  • A player who cannot follow may play any card, including a trump.
  • The highest card of the suit led wins the trick, unless a trump was played: then the highest trump wins.
  • The winner of a trick leads to the next one.

Who wins

If declarer takes at least the number of tricks bid, the declaring side scores; if not, the defenders score for every trick short. Bidding and making a game (3NT, 4♥, 4♠, 5♣ or 5♦) or a slam (12 or 13 tricks) earns a big bonus.

Counting your hand

Count high-card points: ace 4, king 3, queen 2, jack 1 (40 in the pack). With 12 or more you usually open the bidding. As a rule of thumb a partnership with 25 points can make a game and with 33 a small slam.
