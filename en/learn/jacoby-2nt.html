Jacoby 2NT: the Game-Forcing Raise of a Major, with Examples

Jacoby 2NT over 1♥ or 1♠ shows four-card support and game values. Opener’s rebids (shortness, strong or minimum hand) and an example.

The idea

After partner opens 1♥ or 1♠, a jump to 2NT shows four or more cards in partner’s major and at least 13 points. The partnership is forced to game, and the room below game is used to look for a slam.

Opener’s rebids

  • 3 of a new suit: a singleton or void in that suit.
  • 4 of a new suit: a good five-card side suit.
  • 3 of the major: 18+ points, no shortness.
  • 3NT: 15–17 points, no shortness.
  • 4 of the major: a minimum (12–14), no shortness.

Why it helps

A singleton opposite partner’s small cards is worth a lot; opposite honours such as K-Q-x it is worth little. Responder can now judge whether the hands fit for a slam.

Example

Partner opens 1♠ and you hold ♠KQ74 ♥A5 ♦KJ83 ♣Q92 (15 points). Bid 2NT. Over 3♥ (short hearts) only your ace sits in that suit, so a slam is possible; over 4♠ (a minimum) you pass.
