Bridge Scoring Explained: Tricks, Games, Slams, IMP and MP

How a bridge score is worked out: trick points, part scores, games, slams, doubles, undertricks, and how IMP and matchpoints compare your result with the field.

Trick points

For every trick above six that was bid and made: 20 in clubs or diamonds, 30 in hearts or spades, and in notrump 40 for the first and 30 for each further trick. Doubled they count twice, redoubled four times.

Games and part scores

A contract worth 100 trick points or more is a game: 3NT, 4♥, 4♠, 5♣, 5♦ or higher. In duplicate a made game earns 300 not vulnerable and 500 vulnerable; a smaller contract (a part score) earns 50.

Slams

A small slam (12 tricks) adds 500 (750 vulnerable), a grand slam (13 tricks) 1000 (1500 vulnerable), on top of the game bonus.

Going down

For each trick short the defenders score 50 (100 vulnerable). Doubled: 100, 300, 500 and then 300 more for each further trick not vulnerable; 200, 500, 800 and 300 more each vulnerable.

Examples

  • 4♠ making exactly: 120 + 300 = 420 (620 vulnerable).
  • 3NT making: 100 + 300 = 400 (600 vulnerable).
  • 2♥ making with an overtrick: 90 + 50 = 140.
  • 6♥ making: 180 + 300 + 500 = 980 (1430 vulnerable).

IMP and matchpoints

In duplicate bridge the same boards are played at several tables, so luck in the cards cancels out. Matchpoints (MP) compare your score with every other table: you get a point for each table you beat and half for each tie, shown as a percentage. IMP turns the difference between two scores into a number on a scale: 20–40 points is 1 IMP, 50–80 is 2, 90–120 is 3, … 370–420 is 9, 430–490 is 10, up to 24 IMP for a difference of 4000 or more.
