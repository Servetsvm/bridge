Stayman Convention: How It Works, with Examples

Stayman over a 1NT opening: 2♣ asks partner for a four-card major. The answers, what responder bids next, and when to use it, with examples.

The idea

When partner opens 1NT (15–17 points, balanced), a 4-4 fit in a major usually plays better than notrump. 2♣ asks: do you hold a four-card major? It says nothing about clubs.

Opener's answers

  • 2♦: no four-card major.
  • 2♥: four hearts (may also hold four spades).
  • 2♠: four spades, not four hearts.

When to use it

Responder needs at least one four-card major and usually 8 or more points, enough to invite game. With a five-card major use a transfer instead.

Responder's next bid

  • A fit: raise to 3 (invitation) or 4 (game) of the major.
  • No fit: 2NT invites, 3NT is game.
  • After 2♥ responder's 3NT may still hide four spades: opener with four spades then bids 4♠.

Example

Partner opens 1NT and you hold ♠KJ74 ♥Q52 ♦A83 ♣965 (10 points). Bid 2♣. Over 2♠ bid 4♠; over 2♦ or 2♥ bid 3NT (partner corrects to 4♠ with four spades).

Weak Stayman

With a very weak hand short in clubs and both majors (for example 4-4-4-1) you can bid 2♣ and pass any answer: 2♦, 2♥ or 2♠ will play better than 1NT.
